One small word runs this whole unit. That word is because.
It tells your reason.

This unit joins two things. English and science.
Science looks at the Earth.
You will learn how to care for it.
You will also do Christmas things.
Sing songs. Make a card. Write to Santa.
And you will do a chant with actions.

Now the star word.
Two small ideas. I like Christmas. It is fun.
Put because in the middle.
One sentence now. And it tells why.

Here is a trap.
I like Christmas. It is fun.
I like Christmas because it is fun.
Which one tells the reason?...
Both are fine. But the second one joins the two ideas.
Joining and telling why. That is what you learn here.

Another trap.
I help Mummy because I love she.
Sounds close. But it is wrong.
After the action word, say her.
I love her.
Remember this spot. You will learn it again soon.

One more.
Because he is kind.
Can a sentence start like this?...
No. Because needs a full sentence in front.
Santa gives gifts because he is kind.
First the action. Then because. Then the reason.

Try it with your blocks.
Two blocks. One small joiner in the middle.
No joiner. Two blocks sit apart.
With the joiner. One long piece.
Because is that joiner.
In the test, write: We use because to tell the reason.

In secondary school, this joiner gets a name. Conjunction.
A conjunction is a joining word.
Because joins two sentences into one.
You already know one conjunction. Because.

The letter asks four things.
Do you speak politely?...
Do you try your best?...
Are you caring?...
Do you look after your own things?...
Ask yourself. Tick only what is true.
No one else can tick for you.

Soon you will perform the chant. With actions and a big voice.

Four sounds sit on the top of this page. Ch. Sh. Th. Ng.
Ten words sit under them.
These ten words will come up in your spelling test.

First trap. Which sound starts chip... ch or sh...
Chip starts with ch. Sh is the wind sound. Ship starts with sh.
Lots of children mix them up.
Listen to the first sound only.

Next trap. Which sound ends sing... ng or n...
Sing ends with ng. Your mouth stays open.
Sun ends with n. Your tongue touches the top.
Say both. Feel the difference.

Look at th. The page says voiced and unvoiced.
That means th has two sounds.
Think has no buzz. This has a buzz.
Put your hand on your throat. Say this. Feel it.
So th always sounds the same. True or false...
False. Two sounds.

Last trap. Which word starts with the w sound... when or who...
Trick question.
Who starts with w on paper. But you do not hear the w.
Just say who. When has the w sound.

In your workbook, write: Ch and sh each have two letters but one sound. Th has two sounds. Ng and n end words.

Now a big-school name. Digraph.
Two letters join together. They make one new sound.
Ch is a digraph. Sh is a digraph.
Think of two friends. They hold hands. They walk as one.
C and h are two letters. They hold hands. You do not say c then h.
Every sound on this page works this way. Ch. Sh. Th. Ng.
That is why you blend words with them.

Three test words. Say them out loud. When. Where. Because.
Say each word before you write it.
Tonight, cover the words. Write them from memory.

A verb is a doing word.
Six phrases sit at the top. Some words do. Some words are things.
How do you spot a doing word?
Ask yourself. Can I do this?...
If yes, it is a verb.

Turkey is food. Can you turkey? No.
Eat is the doing. Eat gets the line.

This catches many children.
In this task, the verb is one doing word.
Kiss is the doing. Grandma is the person.
The words after the verb tell you more.

Cake. Grandpa. Cut.

One more thing. In secondary school, you will learn a new name. Past tense.
It means the action is finished. The verb changes shape.
Today I jump. Yesterday I jumped.

I jumped. I played. I laughed.
Every finished action gets a new shape.

Countable nouns. Uncountable nouns.
Countable means you can count one by one.
One bell. Two bells.
Uncountable means you cannot.
Tinsel has no one, no two.
You just say some tinsel.
You already use this rule. Just without the names.

First trap. I want a ball because I want a ball.
This looks like one sentence. But it is empty.
Read the part after because. Ask, why?

A full stop cuts the sentence. Then because hangs alone.

Three parts here. What you liked. What is easier. What you will do for the Earth.

First question. What did you enjoy most?... Two boxes wait. Think of what made you smile. Then colour that box. Only you know.

Next question. Which is easier?... Think. Which one felt light for you?... Colour that one. Your pick is true for you.

Third question is the big one. What will you do this Christmas for the Earth?... Two boxes show two good plans. Colour the one you will really do. Any plan that helps the Earth is good.

Now say it out loud. Say what you will do. Then say because. Then say why. Remember how because works. Before because, the action. After because, the reason. You learned this with your letter to Santa.

Here is a trap. I care for the Earth. That is the start. Then comes because. I throw away my old decorations. I buy new ones. Good sentence or not?... The shape is right. But the reason is wrong. Throw away makes more trash. That does not help the Earth. The reason must truly help the Earth.

In your workbook, write: Say what you will do. Say because. Say why.

Try it with your own plan. Pick one plan from the boxes. Say the action. Say because. Say your own reason. That is one whole promise.

Last part. Sounds. Five little boxes. ch. sh. th voiced. th unvoiced. ng and n.

Say each sound first. Then write it with your finger on the table. Can you say it and write it? Colour it. Not yet? Leave it white. That is fine too.

One trap hides here. True or false: th has one sound only... False. There are two th boxes. Voiced means your throat shakes. Unvoiced means no shake. Put your hand on your throat. Say them. Feel the difference.

Another trap. Ng and n are the same sound. True or false?... False. Two sounds. One box holds both because they get mixed up.

Your unit is now one whole thing. Christmas fun. Earth care. And because to tell why.
